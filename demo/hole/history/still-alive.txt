Gopher is not quite dead

Gopher never truly disappeared. It only went quiet. A small community still
keeps servers running, often called gopherholes or phlogs (Gopher blogs), like
lights left on in an abandoned town. Floodgap, run by Cameron Kaiser, has
been their gathering place for years and keeps a list of the servers that
still answer.

Browsers mostly turned their backs on it. Firefox removed built-in Gopher
support in version 4, in 2011. But the text browser Lynx still speaks Gopher,
and so does curl: you can summon a menu with a plain curl gopher:// command.

In 2019 a new protocol called Gemini rose from the same ground, openly
inspired by Gopher. It keeps the simple, text-first spirit and adds
encryption. Many people who keep gopherholes also keep Gemini capsules.

To raise your own hole, all you need is a folder of text files and a tiny
server. Port 70 is the traditional Gopher port, the door the old clients
still knock on.
