The birth of Gopher

Gopher was written in 1991 at the University of Minnesota by a small team led
by Mark McCahill, with Farhad Anklesaria, Paul Lindner, Daniel Torrey and Bob
Alberti. It was named after the university's mascot, the Golden Gophers, and
after the idea of a "go-fer" that fetches things for you.

The idea was simple. A Gopher server answers one request line with either a
document or a menu. A menu is a list of lines, and each line says what kind of
thing it points to, what to call it, and which server and selector to ask for
it. Clients showed those menus as lists you could arrow through. You could
burrow from one university's server into another's without noticing.

The protocol was published as RFC 1436 in March 1993. It is short enough to
read over a cup of coffee. A whole server fits in a few hundred lines of code,
which is one reason people still run them for fun today.

Gopher spread fast through universities in 1992 and 1993. For a while it was
the most popular way to browse information on the internet.
