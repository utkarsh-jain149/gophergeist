WAIS (around 1990)

WAIS, Wide Area Information Servers, was a system for searching the full text
of documents on remote servers. It was developed around 1990 by Thinking
Machines Corporation together with Apple, Dow Jones and KPMG. Brewster Kahle
led the work at Thinking Machines.

Unlike Archie, WAIS searched what was inside documents, and it ranked results
by relevance. Gopher and early web servers often linked to WAIS databases
for searching.

Brewster Kahle went on to found the Internet Archive in 1996, the great
keeper of the dead web. It is the reason so much of the early internet can
still be visited, long after its servers went dark.

Favorite food on the altar: relevance-ranked results, still warm.
