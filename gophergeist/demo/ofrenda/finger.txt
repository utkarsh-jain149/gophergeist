finger (1971)

finger let you reach out to a user on another computer and learn whether they
were logged in, when they last read their mail, and the contents of their
.plan and .project files. Les Earnest wrote the first version around 1971 at
the Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory. It was later written up as
RFC 742 in 1977 and RFC 1288 in 1991.

People turned their .plan files into tiny blogs long before blogs existed.
Game programmer John Carmack famously posted development notes in his .plan
file, and fans would finger his account to read them.

finger faded because it told strangers too much about the people it watched,
and because a bug in one finger server was the door the Morris worm crept
through in 1988.

Favorite food on the altar: a .plan file that says "nothing planned".
